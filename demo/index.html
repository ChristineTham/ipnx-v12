ipnx-v12

a reimagining of Unix — the counterfactual next Research edition, running in this tab

A modified Plan 9 kernel hosted as an ordinary process, with per-process namespaces, 9P as the only IPC, and WebAssembly as the executable format. What boots below is not a tribute: the shell is the real 4th-edition rc, the editors are the real sam and acme (all twenty source files, verbatim), twenty-four real commands ride the real Plan 9 libraries, V10 binaries from the TUHS tapes sit in /v10/bin — and real Go and CPython 3.14 binaries run against the same kernel through a WASI shim.

Boot the desktop — shells, sam, acme Run the 132-test suite

Once the prompt appears, take the guided tour:

rc /rc/tour

Or go straight for the good parts: win acme & opens the real acme in a window (button-2 executes, button-3 searches); @{bind /lib/alt /etc; cat /etc/motd} shows a subshell rearranging its own private namespace; /v10/bin/echo -e 'a\nb' | wc pipes 1989 into 1992.

Everything runs in your browser — nothing you type leaves this tab, and reloading forgets it all. Measured green in Chromium (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc) and in Safari — the full 132-test suite passes in both. (Safari needed real engineering: WebKit deterministically fails concurrent module-worker loads through a service worker — minimal repro and file-ready report in the repository under demo/webkit-repro/ — so this page serializes worker startup.) Firefox is untested. The whole system — kernel, sources, the 132-test conformance suite, and the documents that argue for it — is at github.com/ChristineTham/ipnx-v12. Third-party licences: NOTICES.
